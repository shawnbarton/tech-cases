# Analysis of runs 001–002 and the DP-2 static (2026-10-20)

Sources: KWin v6.7.5 / v6.8.0 / master (github.com/KDE/kwin mirror), NVIDIA open-gpu-kernel-modules 580.178.04 and 615.78.08, Linux v7.2. Two research passes (KWin source; NVIDIA and kernel source; web; the CSV data), each finding then put to three skeptical reviewers. Below, "580" and "615" mean those trees. File:line references are from them.

## Late frames (stutter)

What the KWin frame log columns mean (6.7.5):
- `target pageflip timestamp` is the render loop's original target. The commit thread's rescheduled target is never logged (`core/renderbackend.cpp:46-51`, `backends/drm/drm_commit_thread.cpp:89-96`). A frame the commit thread pushed back still shows as one refresh late.
- `pageflip timestamp` is the DRM event timestamp. nvidia-drm never calls `drm_vblank_init` (580, and 615 unless `nvidia-drm.vblank=1`), so the kernel stamps it with `ktime_get()` when nvidia-drm sends the event (`drm_vblank.c:1147-1165`).
- The event reaches nvidia-drm after two hops on the `nvidia-modeset/kthread_q` thread and two takes of the global `nvkms_lock`.
- `render end` is the union of the CPU paint span, the Intel GL timer query (2 ms floor) and the multi-GPU copy query (`core/renderbackend.cpp:67-85`, `opengl/glrendertimequery.cpp:46-91`). On the Vulkan copy path it leaves out the NVIDIA queue's wait on the Intel fence, and the time until NVIDIA signals the copy's fence (`multigpuswapchain.cpp:200-203, 269-279`, `vulkan/vulkan_render_time_query.cpp:26-54`).

How KWin 6.7.5 commits:
- The commit thread sleeps until target − safety margin.
- It then polls the scanout buffer's sync fd (`drm_buffer.cpp:93-106`). If the fence isn't signalled yet, the target moves one refresh later.
- No IN_FENCE_FD is attached on NVIDIA in 6.7.5 (`drm_commit.cpp:80-83`). 6.8.0 attaches it (commit d6a6bd6718).
- Commit N waits until the main thread has handled flip event N−1 (`drm_commit_thread.cpp:42-74, 374-387`).
- The margin is vblank time + 1000 µs (`KWIN_DRM_OVERRIDE_SAFETY_MARGIN` replaces the 1000 µs), plus an adaptive part of up to 3 ms (`:150-180, 362-372`).

NVIDIA side:
- The commit ioctl never waits for fences or the flip. Plane fences become display semaphores, so a late fence shows up as a late latch, which KWin can't see (`nvidia-drm-modeset.c`, `__nv_drm_convert_in_fences`).
- NVIDIA dma_fences have no `.signaled` op. A `poll()` therefore only turns readable once the interrupt-driven callback has run (`nvidia-drm-fence.c:137-143, 1354-1377`).
- One global `nvkms_lock` serialises both heads' commits, flip-event delivery, and DP interrupt and hotplug work.
- Cursor-only commits complete at once, with an event stamped at commit time. They make no hardware flip (`nvidia-drm-modeset.c:95-136, 736-772`).

What the data shows (runs 001 and 002):
- Flips land within ~30 µs of the vblank grid, and late ones exactly one vblank late. That points to a late latch rather than jittery reporting. A report delayed by exactly one vblank isn't excluded until the trace compares NVKMS flip-done with the commit (or 615 runs with `nvidia-drm.vblank=1`).
- About half of the late frames are knock-ons of an earlier late frame.
- Pooled, most root late frames are the first frame after one or more skipped vblanks. Those are late 24–43% of the time (28/24% in run 001, 41/43% in run 002), against 1–2% of back-to-back frames. The rate doesn't grow with pause length (0/7 after more than 0.5 s), so a deep power-state wake-up doesn't fit.
- Many of these frames rendered nothing new: their span is under 2 ms, so no compositing and no copy. The copy fence can't explain them, and you can't see them.
- Some on-time frames have targets 1–2 ms before the true vblank. That looks like KWin re-timing from cursor-only commit events.
- Back-to-back late frames often follow a flip event that arrived 0.3–1.5 ms late. They carry the "both monitors late together" pattern, which is about 10× chance.
- The KWin safety margin rarely rises after a late frame, which argues against slow commit ioctls.
- The paint-span evidence for KWin main-thread stalls didn't hold up. Main-thread uploads of Qt Widgets (wl_shm) buffers remain possible: udmabuf import is off by default on Intel and NVIDIA (`opengl/egldisplay.cpp:393-407`).
- DP-2 and DP-3 run slightly different modes (refresh 16 667 777 ns vs 16 667 500 ns), so the heads aren't raster-locked.

Candidates, and the test that separates each:
- NVIDIA latches the first flip after a pause late, and needs more lead time: a larger `KWIN_DRM_OVERRIDE_SAFETY_MARGIN`.
- Cursor-only commit events re-time KWin: `KWIN_FORCE_SW_CURSOR=1`.
- Flip events delivered late through `nvkms_lock` / kthread: a larger margin, and the trace.
- The fence not yet signalled at commit time: the trace, and KWin 6.8.0.

The trace (`testbed/trace/flipfence.bt`) is the most direct test, and it changes nothing on the system. Avoid `KWIN_DRM_DISABLE_BUFFER_READABILITY_CHECKS` and `KWIN_DISABLE_VULKAN` alone on 6.7.5: either can scan out unfinished copies, which would look like the static.

- [#650](https://github.com/NVIDIA/open-gpu-kernel-modules/issues/650) is tracked as NVIDIA bug 4830125 (mtijanic, 2024-10-09), still open. No cause or fix has been published.

## The static on DP-2

- It isn't random link errors. The DP 8b/10b scrambler resets only at every 512th BS, and BS comes once per line. With Vtotal 2222 (4K60 CVT-RB), 1125 or 2250 the same pixels scramble differently every frame. Random bit errors would twinkle anywhere, independent of content.
- It also appeared at 1080p60 (case record §4), which likely runs at a lower lane rate (RBR, about 86% full at 30bpp), and at 4K30 (about half full). That argues against signal integrity and against bandwidth.
- The cable swap ruled out only the cable and the monitor. These all stay tied to the connector: the NVIDIA head and output, the Maple Ridge DP-in adapter and tunnel, and the Titan Ridge DP-out port.
- What fits a window-tied, persistent pattern:
  - something deterministic upstream of the link encoder: NVIDIA's 30bpp output for that connector, or how the tunnel or dock handles 30-bit data;
  - bad pixels kept in the scanned-out picture until that area is redrawn;
  - an event triggered by redraws.
- Compressed scanout is impossible through nvidia-drm, which refuses compressed framebuffers. KWin uses no overlay planes on NVIDIA in 6.7.5. COSMIC and dGPU-only mode rule out KWin and the copy.

The 8 bpc test:
- The kernel EDID override reaches NVKMS. Source: `drm_edid.c:2246-2261, 2333-2351`; `nvidia-drm-connector.c:103-117` (615: 150-164); `nvkms-kapi.c:1499-1510`; `nvkms-dpy.c:191-213, 245-262`.
- For DP with EDID 1.4, `rgb444.maxBpc` follows EDID byte 20 (`nvkms-dpy.c:3373-3420`). Nothing requests a lower bpc, so NVKMS picks that maximum unless the bandwidth check fails (`nvkms-modeset.c:290-318`, `nvkms-evo.c` ChooseColorBpc).
- The new depth applies only at a full modeset. KWin doesn't do one on an EDID property change (`drm_gpu.cpp:304-330`). Hence the mode switch in `edid-8bpc.sh`.
- Proof is `/proc/driver/nvidia-modeset/heads` (`nvkms.c:6070-6180`), which shows `30bpp 4:4:4` or `24bpp 4:4:4`. The sysfs EDID only shows DRM's copy.
- At 8 bpc NVKMS also turns dithering on (`nvkms-evo.c:3315-3352`). A cure therefore implicates "30bpp output on this path", not bpc alone.
- debugfs `edid_override` fails under Secure Boot lockdown, and its reset needs exactly `printf reset`. The runtime `drm.edid_firmware` parameter (`/sys/module/drm/parameters/edid_firmware`) works in both cases. RPM Fusion keeps nvidia out of the initramfs, so firmware files in `/usr/lib/firmware/edid` load fine.
- 615 adds `nvidia-modeset.max_output_color_depth` (6/8/10/12; `nvidia-modeset-linux.c:95-96`, `nvkms-dpy.c:3603-3622`). It doesn't exist in 580–610.
- `nvidia-modeset.debug` (580: 0600) puts DP link interrupt and retrain messages in the kernel log. Use it to check whether blanks are link loss.

Prior reports:
- NVIDIA forum thread 179297 #22: Linux-only corruption with 4K monitors behind a USB-C dock. It went away with a DP→HDMI adapter, which forces 24-bit. That likely changed the link type too.
- Forum 155374: window-dependent static on a Thunderbolt dock was a real NVIDIA driver bug, but with MST, and fixed around 2021.
- Forum 382464 (610.43.02): DP blanking and sparkles fixed with an 8 bpc EDID. Search snippet only, which says drm.edid_firmware and debugfs were ignored and only NVIDIA's own `config_file` override worked. That's a reason to check `nv-heads` after `try`.
- KDE bug 526953 (2026-10-11): "Color resolution: unknown" on NVIDIA DP. Closed as not a bug: NVIDIA exposes no `max bpc`.

Not verified: the VESA scrambler text itself; whether the TB3 tunnel keeps the source link rate or strips stuffing; whether KWin uses the hardware cursor plane on these heads; which multi-GPU copy path (Vulkan or EGL) this session uses; NVIDIA release-note text (nvidia.com was unreachable).
